# Decisions

- 2026-10-14 Package name: rail_risk, src layout.
- 2026-10-14 Routes: BHM to EUS, BHM to MAN, and AST to EUS via BHM.
- 2026-10-14 Repo will be public on GitHub later.
- 2026-10-14 Terraform is plan-only, no AWS.
- 2026-10-14 Interval method: LightGBM quantile models with conformalized quantile regression; fallback empirical residual quantiles per route and hour.
- 2026-10-14 gitleaks via system binary plus full-history CI scan.
- 2026-10-14 Repo public on GitHub; commits use the noreply email.
- 2026-10-14 CI runners pinned to ubuntu-24.04 so the 2026-10-19 ubuntu-latest switch to Ubuntu 26 cannot break CI unannounced.
- 2026-10-15 Cancellation labelling unresolved: no cancelled service appeared in 25 sampled BHM to EUS services, so HSP cancellation representation is unknown. Reason codes are numeric strings on late stops. Needs a larger sample using one hour windows.
- 2026-10-15 Darwin captured unfiltered and resumable via committed offsets; route filtering in Spark.
- 2026-10-15 HSP backfill cost, measured on bhm-eus for 2026-09-30 to 2026-10-02, hours 06-22: 48 windows and 222 services (74 per weekday) took 593 s over 274 HTTP calls (48 metrics, 222 details, 4 retries), about 2.2 s per call at the 1 s gap. Result: 2,574 stop rows, 0 quarantined, 3 candidate cancellations. One window hit HTTP 503 three times, finished on rerun, and a further rerun made 0 calls.
- 2026-10-15 HSP backfill projection: one route-day is about 90 calls and 200 s, so 12 months by 3 routes (1,095 route-days) is about 99,000 calls and 60 hours of API time, an upper bound because weekends carry fewer services and ast-eus rids shared with bhm-eus are fetched once. Volume per day is untested outside these 3 weekdays.
- 2026-10-15 Recommended backfill span: 6 months, bhm-eus and bhm-man first, in 30 day chunks overnight (about 20 hours for those two, 30 for all three), then extend to 12 months only if the delay model needs the seasonality.
- 2026-10-15 HSP route membership: a rid is fetched once and linked to every route that returns it (rid_routes), so reruns for another route add membership without refetching.
- 2026-10-15 HSP timestamps are stored as UTC, converted from Europe/London. Ambiguous local times (clocks back) take the first occurrence unless that would run backwards within the service, then the second. Non-existent local times (clocks forward) use the pre-change offset, which shifts them forward by the gap.
- 2026-10-15 Stage 2 summary: HSP and Darwin both load into validated Parquet with write-once raw files, a manifest per source, UTC timestamps and a quarantine table that keeps rejected rows. HSP: 12 months by 3 routes costs about 60 hours of API time, backfill of bhm-eus and bhm-man running newest first. Darwin: about 1.2 MB per minute stored, parsed at 3 to 5 s per hourly file, unfiltered, with route filtering left to Spark. Cancellation labels will come from Darwin schedule cancelReason and per-stop can flags, checked against HSP candidate_cancelled.
- 2026-10-15 Features: PySpark 4.2.0 on JDK 17, local[4] with 6 GB driver. Snapshots are one row per service, route and horizon (T-120, T-60, T-15) at T = scheduled destination arrival minus the horizon. Live state uses only actuals at or before T, historical features use only services dated 1 to 28 days earlier, and leakage tests (synthetic and 50 real rows) must stay at zero mismatches. Interim HSP files are normalised through Arrow before Spark reads them, because pandas writes nanosecond timestamps and null-typed columns that Spark rejects. Superseded 2026-10-16: six horizons, 5, 15, 30, 60, 90 and 120 minutes, with minutes_to_arrival as a feature.
- 2026-10-15 Removed reason_share_passed from the snapshot features: HSP reason codes come from the final record with no attach time, so they can leak and would not match live serving. Bank holidays now come from config/bank_holidays.json, fetched from gov.uk (not typed from memory); the earlier hard-coded list matched it for 2025 and 2026.
- 2026-10-15 Evaluation: time-based split by service date over complete days (test = latest 7, validation = 7 before, 1 day gap each, train earlier), config/split.yaml. Delay bands 0-14, 15-29, 30-59, 60-119, 120+ are provisional (config/delay_bands.yaml). Metrics carry 95% bootstrap intervals from 1,000 resamples of whole service dates, seed 20261015. Baselines are fitted on the training block only. With 8 training days so far, the historical baseline's residual quantiles are thin and will be refit as the backfill adds older dates.
- 2026-10-15 Stage 4 pipeline: LightGBM quantile models (0.1, 0.5, 0.9) with horizon as a feature and conformalized quantile regression per horizon, plus a LightGBM band model calibrated by isotonic one-vs-rest or temperature scaling, whichever has the lower cross-validated (by service date) log loss on validation. Validation tunes, early-stops the median model and calibrates; with 10 or more validation days it is split in two by date. Results are tagged smoke and no report is written until train has 60 complete days (config/train.yaml). MLflow uses the file store at mlruns/, which MLflow 3 calls maintenance mode and opens only with MLFLOW_ALLOW_FILE_STORE=true.
- 2026-10-15 Stage 4.2 conformal: Mondrian CQR, offsets per horizon and observed lateness group at T (not started, 0-4, 5-14, 15+ minutes, from last_stop_lateness_min only, never the label). A group with fewer than 30 calibration rows merges into its neighbour with fewer rows and the merge is logged. The global per-horizon CQR is kept as a comparison column. Reason: 4.1 coverage was 0.28 on trains 15 to 59 minutes late.
- 2026-10-15 Stage 4.2 target: direct delay and residual (delay minus lateness at T, zero when not started) are each fitted with their own 12-combination grid, and the target with the lower tuning pinball loss is kept per horizon. That is 24 fits in total; the 12 cap in 4.1 applied per target. Pinball loss is identical on either scale, so the comparison is exact.
- 2026-10-15 Stage 4.2 drift: with 10 or more validation days the later half calibrates and the earlier half tunes (already in place). split.yaml keeps validation at 7 days, so the halving does not run on the real data yet; 14 validation days would need 83 complete days for 60 train days.
- 2026-10-15 MLflow now uses a sqlite backend at mlruns/mlflow.db with artifacts under mlruns/artifacts, both git-ignored. The MLFLOW_ALLOW_FILE_STORE workaround is removed. The 4.1 smoke run stays in the old file store and was not migrated.

- 2026-10-15 Spark: spark.driver.host is pinned to 127.0.0.1 beside bindAddress. Without it a cached block was fetched from the LAN address and refused, which failed the real-data leakage check.
- 2026-10-15 Metric change: coverage by true delay band is descriptive only and no longer a pass or fail check. An interval fixed before the outcome is known under-covers when rows are selected by that outcome, so the 0.70 band flag was a flawed target. The checks are coverage by observed lateness at T (the conformal guarantee) and reliability of P(delay at least 15, 30, 60) from the band model (Brier, ECE with 10 equal-count bins, reliability table, date bootstrap CIs), with the historical band frequencies as the bar. MAE gains are tested with a paired date bootstrap against the bar.
- 2026-10-15 Provenance: rail-risk train tags a run reportable only on a clean git tree with at least min_train_days, and logs the HEAD sha. A dirty tree downgrades the run to smoke with a warning and writes no report.
- 2026-10-15 Split: validation is now 14 days (config/split.yaml) once 83 complete days exist, so with 10 or more validation days the earlier 7 tune and the later 7 calibrate. The test block is unchanged.
- 2026-10-15 Threshold calibration: P(delay at least 15) and P(at least 30) from the band model are recalibrated after band calibration with isotonic regression or Platt scaling, both fitted on the calibrate half of validation and chosen by summed binary log loss on the tune half (date-fold cross-validated when validation is not split). Band probabilities are rebuilt from the exceedances, each capped by the one below, so they stay monotone, non-negative and sum to 1. P(at least 60) and above are left as the band model gave them. Reason: ECE for P(at least 15) was worse than the historical frequencies at every horizon, and that is the threshold Delay Repay pays on.
- 2026-10-15 Delay Repay rules are in config/delay_repay.yaml by TOC, read only from each operator's own page. VT (Avanti West Coast) is verified from the page, which was last updated 2026-07-21 and checked 2026-10-15. LM (West Midlands Railway) is unverified: the page returned HTTP 403 with a JavaScript security check, and LM also covers London Northwestern Railway, whose page was not checked. An unverified TOC cannot be priced, and the fare is always supplied by the caller.
- 2026-10-15 Backfill order: bhm-eus, then ast-bhm (Aston to Birmingham New Street, the inbound leg for connections), then bhm-man. run_backfill.sh defaults to that ROUTES order. Restarting the runner is safe: finished windows are skipped and only failed or missing windows are fetched.
- 2026-10-15 Threshold calibration is chosen per horizon among none, Platt and isotonic by summed log loss on the tune half (ties to the simpler method), with the methods fitted on the calibrate half. Reason: Platt won by a tiny margin overall and made T-120 worse.
- 2026-10-15 West Midlands Railway (TOC LM) tiers are recorded as verified from the project owner's manual reading of its page on 2026-10-15, after the automated fetch got HTTP 403; the code did not fetch them. LM is shared with London Northwestern Railway, whose page lists no tiers, so London Northwestern Railway is a separate unverified entry and every LM payout carries a caveat. The claim window for LM is not recorded.
- 2026-10-15 Minimum connection time for BHM is 10 minutes marked assumed in config/stations.yaml: National Rail's Birmingham New Street page (checked 2026-10-15) has no interchange time, so it could not be verified.
- 2026-10-15 Connection simulator (src/rail_risk/connection.py): the inbound arrival is drawn from the calibrated 0.05 to 0.95 quantiles of the Stage 4 model (linear interpolation, tails extended by one end segment, so the far right tail is understated), the outbound trains from the empirical (BHM departure delay, EUS arrival delay) pairs by scheduled hour on training dates only, 2,000 draws with a fixed seed. Legs are assumed independent, so shared causes such as signal failures or strikes are not modelled and P(miss) is optimistic when delays are correlated. The extra quantile levels are calibrated with one-sided conformal offsets per horizon and lateness group, with the same group merges as the interval. The model was fitted on bhm-eus only, so its accuracy on the ast-bhm inbound leg is not validated.
- 2026-10-15 config/split.yaml has a routes list (bhm-eus) that defines which routes the split, training and baselines use, so partial ingests of ast-bhm and bhm-man cannot shrink the set of complete days. Superseded 2026-10-16: the list is now bhm-eus and ast-bhm.
- 2026-10-15 Feasibility: a connection is scored only if the scheduled outbound departure minus the scheduled inbound arrival is at least the minimum connection time. With no outbound given the first feasible one is chosen; an infeasible one is refused and the message names the first feasible departure. The backtest builds pairs only inside the feasible window, so no infeasible connection is ever scored.
- 2026-10-15 Minimum connection time: BHM stays 10 minutes marked assumed, and the connection command prints P(miss), the 80% arrival range and the expected payout for 5, 7, 10 and 15 minutes. Correction (2026-10-15): an earlier version of this line said live National Rail sells sub-10-minute changes at BHM. That was inferred from a 7-minute gap between two timetabled trains and was not verified, so it is not evidence about the true minimum connection time. The value of 10 is an assumption in either direction, and the sensitivity table is kept for that reason.
- 2026-10-15 Payout is unconditional over all draws. A stranded draw (no catchable alternative that day) is counted in the top tier (120 or more minutes late), which assumes a stranded passenger claims the longest delay. The 80% arrival range excludes stranded draws and says so. The earlier "conditional on completing" figure is removed because it overstated the payout.
- 2026-10-15 Tail: above the 0.95 quantile the inbound delay is the 0.95 quantile plus a draw from the empirical excess of training outcomes over the calibrated 0.95 quantile, per observed-lateness group (pooled where a group has fewer than 30 excesses), from training dates only. Check on test rows: simulated P(delay at least 60) was within the observed bootstrap CI at every horizon, but only 1 of 432 rows per horizon reached 60 minutes, so the check has almost no power; on validation T-120 predicted 0.0045 against 0.0010 observed (CI 0 to 0.0032), a slight overstatement.
- 2026-10-15 Backtest (rail-risk backtest-connections): pairs are every ast-bhm inbound with each bhm-eus outbound scheduled between the minimum connection time and that plus 20 minutes. Forecast times are 60 and 15 minutes before the outbound departure, which is minus gap plus k minutes relative to the inbound's scheduled arrival; snapshots are built at those horizons and the model is given the nearest trained horizon (120, 60 or 15). The baseline is the historical miss frequency by 5-minute scheduled-gap bucket with the evaluated date left out. The outbound delay history leaves the evaluated date out. First smoke result (one day, baseline in-sample): at T-60 the model predicted a mean miss rate of 0.424 against 0.077 realised, so the bhm-eus model transfers badly to the ast-bhm inbound leg at that horizon; at T-15 it predicted 0.036 against 0.077.
- 2026-10-15 Serving (Stage 6.1): every training run now logs a serving bundle (quantile and band models, calibrators, the historical baseline) next to its boosters. `rail-risk promote --run-id` registers a reportable run as model "connection-risk" and moves the alias "champion" in the sqlite store; the API loads by alias, so a retrain only moves the alias. The bundle is a pickle written by this project and loaded only from its own store. Run daa8c118 had no bundle, so ccaafb52 (same sha lineage, same split, identical test MAE) was registered instead.
- 2026-10-15 Live state: Darwin stop state comes from the parsed tables plus a read-only tolerant read of raw hourly files not yet in the parse manifest, deduplicated on (partition, offset). TIPLOCs for BHM, EUS, AST and MAN map to CRS via config/tiplocs.yaml, matched by name only. Features are built by the training `build_snapshots` function on a stop table in the HSP schema, with the 28-day history labels computed once at startup. A Spark job costs 1 to 2 seconds, so features for every active service are recomputed in one batch every 30 seconds and requests read the cache, with an on-demand build on a miss and a 2 second budget that returns 504 rather than a partial answer.
- 2026-10-15 Fallbacks are visible in data_quality: Darwin data older than 10 minutes treats the service as not started (stale_live_data); a model that fails to load serves the historical baseline (model_fallback), and the timetable if there is no baseline either; an unverified or unknown TOC returns no amount with the reason; a cancelled service returns status cancelled with its reason code; an unknown rid is 404. The champion model has fixed horizons of 120, 60 and 15 minutes, so the real minutes to arrival are snapped to the nearest and the response says so; a continuous-horizon model takes the minutes as they are without code changes.
- 2026-10-15 Parity check: Darwin capture began on 2026-10-15, so there is no yesterday to compare. HSP does serve same-day data, so the check used today's bhm-eus services present in both feeds. HSP actuals derive from the same Darwin feed, so agreement is a data-consistency check, not independent verification.
- 2026-10-15 CI determinism: no test asserts a wall-clock time. The timeout path is tested with a feature lookup that blocks until released, and latency is measured by scripts/bench_api.py, which is not run in CI. The first Stage 6 CI run failed because a cold Spark build exceeded the 2 second budget on a shared runner, which is the reason.
- 2026-10-15 Cache misses: a request for a service whose features are not cached never runs Spark on the request path. The rid goes to the front of the refresher queue, the refresher is woken, and the answer is 202 with Retry-After and status warming. 504 now means a genuine timeout on the cached path. A rid whose build produces nothing is remembered for two minutes and answered 404 instead of warming forever. Features for queued rids are built in a batch of their own ahead of the full batch.
- 2026-10-15 Cold start: the Darwin state, the Spark session with history labels, and the model load run concurrently. Measured on this Mac, startup went from 22.3 s to 17.7 s concurrently and to 10.3 s once `rail-risk parse-darwin` has parsed the closed hours, because reading three unparsed raw hours is about 8 s of JSON parsing. The 28-day history labels and a per-rid event index are built once.
- 2026-10-15 UI: the Streamlit page calls the API over HTTP only and imports nothing from the model layer (a test checks the source). It lists services from GET /services, shows the 80% range in large type, the three late-arrival bars, expected Delay Repay when a fare is typed in, and a badge for each data-quality condition. Connections are not exposed until the Step 5.4 retrain. Origins are shown as the Darwin location code, which is a truncated TIPLOC for stations outside tiplocs.yaml.
- 2026-10-15 Screenshot: the brief expected a preinstalled Chromium, but none was installed, so Playwright's headless Chromium (99 MiB) was downloaded into the user cache for the one screenshot. It is not a project dependency.
- 2026-10-15 Containers: one Dockerfile with two targets. The api target is python:3.12-slim-bookworm, libgomp1 for LightGBM, a 57 MB jlink Java 17 runtime for Spark (Spark 4.2 also needs java.desktop and jdk.incubator.vector in it), a locked uv install without the dev and ui groups, test directories removed from the venv, and a non-root user. Measured by the filesystem inside the container: 1.69 GB with bytecode and the Debian JRE, 1.43 GB without bytecode or test directories, 1.28 GB with the jlink runtime. `docker images` on Colima reports the same images 1.6 times larger (2.61, 2.26 and 2.06 GB). The ui target is 175 MB compressed and carries only Streamlit and the page.
- 2026-10-15 Compose mounts data/ and mlruns/ read-only, binds ports to 127.0.0.1, uses a read-only root filesystem with an exec tmpfs for /tmp (Spark unpacks snappy there), and takes credentials only from env_file .env on the optional capture profile, which was not run. Checked for the 4 values in .env: none appears in docker history, docker inspect or any file in either image, and neither image has a .env file.
- 2026-10-15 The model registry records the absolute artefact path of the training machine, so load_alias falls back to the same path under the mounted tracking directory. The bank holiday file is found under the working directory when the package is installed.
- 2026-10-15 CI container job: builds the api image with buildx and the GitHub Actions cache, writes a synthetic data/ and registered model with scripts/make_fixture_store.py inside the image, runs the container with those mounted read-only, and requires /health to report the champion alias with model_fallback false. Real data is never in CI.
- 2026-10-15 Terraform (infra/): provider pinned to hashicorp/aws 5.100.0, no defaults for anything account-specific, 16 resources, plan-only. `terraform plan` needs some credential source to start the provider, so the offline plan uses offline_plan = true (skip credentials validation, account id, metadata check, region validation) with throwaway AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID and AWS_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY set to the word "offline" on the command line. Nothing is applied and costs were not estimated. Starting the task does not yet copy artefacts from S3 into the volumes.
- 2026-10-15 Drift: PSI per feature with 10 quantile bins from training and a bin for missing values, categorical features by training category plus other, thresholds 0.1 watch and 0.25 act. Live features are built 60 minutes before arrival from Darwin with the serving feature code and compared with training rows at the same horizon. First run: Darwin capture started today, so the live sample was 53 services on one day. Day of week, history count and history median show PSI of 4 to 8 from the window and the backfill depth, not from a model problem, and toc_code is 17% missing live because no schedule message was seen for some services, which is a real data-quality finding.
- 2026-10-16 Step 5.4 gate: ast-bhm had 161 complete days inside the train block (60 needed), after a retry pass over ast-bhm and bhm-eus cleared every partial day with no failed window. Five bhm-eus days, three ast-bhm days and one bhm-man day are complete with zero services (for example 2026-05-30 and 2026-05-31 on both routes); they are kept as complete days and contribute no rows. Whether those are real closures was not checked.
- 2026-10-16 Horizons: snapshots are built at 5, 15, 30, 60, 90 and 120 minutes before the scheduled arrival at each route's own destination, with `minutes_to_arrival` as a numeric feature and `route_id` as a category, so one model is trained over bhm-eus and ast-bhm (config/split.yaml routes). The history features were already per route and 28 days. This replaces the three fixed horizons, whose meaning did not transfer between a 80 minute leg and a 7 minute leg. Serving gives the model the real minutes, so nothing is snapped; an older bundle that is not marked continuous still snaps.
- 2026-10-16 Mondrian groups are now route crossed with horizon bucket (under 20, 20 to 74, 75 and over minutes) crossed with observed lateness group. A cell under 30 calibration rows merges into its neighbour and is logged, and a pooled set over all routes backs up a route with no rows. Buckets rather than the six grid values keep enough rows per cell; the target choice (direct or residual) and the P(at least 15 or 30) recalibration are per bucket for the same reason.
- 2026-10-16 Connection backtest is out of sample: pairs on test dates are scored, validation pairs are predicted so alert thresholds can be chosen without the test block, and the delay model, tail, outbound history and gap-bucket baseline use training dates only. A forecast is made k minutes before the outbound departs, with the exact minutes to the inbound's scheduled arrival at that moment. If the inbound has already arrived its delay is known; if its scheduled arrival has passed and it is not in, the draws are conditioned on a delay at least that large. The snapshot history features for these forecasts now come from the full history, not only the scored days.
- 2026-10-16 Simulator fix: inbound delay draws are rounded to whole minutes. Data times are whole minutes and a tie between the passenger being ready and the train leaving counts as a catch, but a continuous draw split every tie as half a miss. On the first backtest the model's mean P(miss) was 9.2% against 6.0% realised at T-60. The cause was found by comparing predicted and realised miss rates by gap bucket on validation and test pairs, so those pairs informed this fix, although it has no fitted parameter. The scores before the fix are kept here for that reason: on 748 test pairs, T-60 Brier 0.0546 and ECE 0.0347, T-15 Brier 0.0431 and ECE 0.0394, against a baseline of 0.0528 and 0.0248.
- 2026-10-16 Alert costs live in config/alerts.yaml as assumptions to revisit with product input: a false alert costs 1 and a missed alert costs 5. The thresholds are chosen on validation pairs by minimum expected cost on a 0.01 grid (ties go to the higher threshold), and scored once on test. The Delay Repay alert uses the journey-level P(final arrival at least 15 minutes late) from the simulation, with a stranded passenger counted as late.
- 2026-10-16 Stage 5 summary (run 4c88d55b, commit fc20d82, registered as connection-risk version 2 and promoted to champion; the API loaded it and answered /risk for live rids with no code change beyond the bundle flag): one model over bhm-eus and ast-bhm trained on 159 complete days, tuned and calibrated on 14, scored on 7 (2026-10-09 to 2026-10-15). On bhm-eus the model matches the earlier run daa8c118 within its intervals (T-60 MAE 5.44 against 5.54, T-15 3.54 against 3.86, 80% coverage 0.81 and 0.84), on a split shifted by one day with 61 more training days. On ast-bhm the leg is 7 minutes and almost never 15 minutes late (0.3% of test rows), so the model's gain over the historical baseline is nil at T-120 and T-60 (MAE difference -0.01 and 0.00 minutes, intervals include zero) and small at T-15 (-0.26, against persistence). Connections, 748 test pairs: at T-60 the model's P(miss) Brier is 0.0525 against 0.0528 for the gap-bucket baseline (no real gain) and ECE 0.0235 against 0.0248; at T-15 Brier is 0.0406 against 0.0528, but 448 of those pairs had already arrived, so that gain is mostly the inbound being known. The 80% arrival range covered 0.87 of pairs, over the nominal 0.8, at a mean width of 32 minutes. Alerts under the assumed 1 to 5 cost: tau 0.22 for likely to miss cost 0.224 per test pair against 0.940 for always and 0.301 for never alerting; tau2 0.26 for Delay Repay cost 0.814 against 0.783 for always and 1.085 for never, because the share of pairs with a 15 minute late arrival rose from 13.4% on validation to 21.7% on test. Superseded in part by Step 6.3: the tau2 Delay Repay alert was removed.
- 2026-10-16 Known gaps after Stage 5: the independence of the two legs is not modelled (on gap 10 to 14 minutes the realised miss rate was 18.4% against 20.3% if outbound delay were independent of the inbound's, so independence overstates misses there, the opposite of what the older note on this said); the minimum connection time is assumed; the history has no winter; bhm-man is not in the model. The real-data leakage test failed in about half of the full test runs with a Spark out of memory error, not a leakage mismatch: six horizons over all history no longer fit the 1 GB shared test session. It now checks the newest 45 days of the two routes, 30 rows each, and passed 4 of 4 full-order reruns. The full-history check was run separately in the 6 GB session: 200 rows per route on each of 4 seeds, 0 mismatches.
- 2026-10-16 Step 6.3, Delay Repay alert removed: the tau2 alert (P of a final arrival at least 15 minutes late) is gone from config/alerts.yaml, the backtest command and the report. Eligibility is known for certain once the train has arrived, so a forecast alert is the wrong product, and on test it cost 0.814 per pair against 0.783 for always alerting after the late-arrival rate moved from 13.4% on validation to 21.7%. Expected Delay Repay in pounds stays as information in /risk, /connection and the UI.
- 2026-10-16 Step 6.3, T-15 gain re-scored: 448 of the 748 test pairs had the inbound already arrived at T-15, so the all-pairs number is kept but labelled, and the report adds the 300 pairs whose inbound had not arrived when the forecast was made (a fact known at prediction time). On those, model Brier is 0.1012 (0.0734 to 0.1422) against 0.1304 (0.0956 to 0.1819) for the baseline, intervals overlapping, and model ECE is 0.0813 against 0.0675, so the model is no better calibrated; it predicts 21.3% misses against 15.0% realised. On the 654 validation pairs of the same kind Brier is 0.1114 against 0.1427 and ECE 0.0578 against 0.0501.
- 2026-10-16 Step 6.3, simulator rounding on validation: the tie-rounding fix was first spotted when the first backtest printed validation and test scores together (test T-60: 9.2% predicted against 6.0% realised; validation: 9.7% against 6.7%), and the diagnosis pooled validation, test and training days, so test pairs did inform it. On validation pairs at T-60 mean P(miss) went from 9.7% to 8.3% against 6.7% realised (Brier 0.0561 to 0.0547), and for the tightest gap bucket from 26.6% to 22.7% against 19.3%. The fix reduces the bias on validation but does not remove it; the model still predicts more misses than happen, by about 1.6 points overall and 3.4 in the tightest bucket.
- 2026-10-16 Step 6.3, serving connections: POST /connection (inbound rid, optional outbound departure, fare, ticket) and GET /inbound. It reuses choose_outbound, simulate with whole-minute rounding and the overdue-inbound floor, journey Delay Repay with the stranded assumption stated in the response, the 5, 7, 10 and 15 minute sensitivity, and tau from config/alerts.yaml (alert when the rounded P(miss) is at least tau, so the flag matches the number shown). A refusal (infeasible, cancelled or already left) is a 200 with status refused and the first feasible departure; a cold cache is 202 as in /risk; missing connection inputs are 503. Outbound delays come from every day of bhm-eus history on disk and the tail model is fitted at startup from the bundle's own training rows (note in data_quality if absent); neither is conditioned on today's running. Scanning Darwin for outbound trains was the slow part and caused a 504 on the first container call, so it only looks at services that also call at Euston and caches for 20 seconds; the first call then took 0.9 s and later calls 15 to 45 ms.
- 2026-10-16 Step 6.3, UI: a My connection tab (Aston to Euston via New Street) shows the miss chance in words (1 in n below 35%, tenths from 35% to 50%), an alert banner at or above tau, the Euston range, expected Delay Repay, and an expander with the sensitivity table and assumptions. It calls the API only.
- 2026-10-16 Step 7.2, P(miss) recalibration: on validation pairs whose inbound had not arrived, split by date (earlier half tunes, later half calibrates, 1,164 and 1,160 forecasts), none, Platt and isotonic were compared by tune-half log loss: none 0.2656, Platt 0.2676, isotonic 0.2752. None won, so config/pmiss_calibration.json says method none, tau stays 0.22 and /connection serves the simulator's P(miss) unchanged.
